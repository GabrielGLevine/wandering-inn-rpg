# City Identity Bible — Liscor · Invrisil · Pallass

Fable-authored 2026-07-06 (user question: three cities, how do they
stay DISTINCT?). The governing rule: **a city is not a bigger map — it
is a different VERB.** Each city asks something different of the
player, and every design layer (art, population, sound, mechanics,
quests) answers that verb. When in doubt on any future city content,
derive from the verb line below.

## The one-line verbs

| City | The city asks you to… | The player feels… |
|---|---|---|
| **Liscor** | **BELONG** | known — a face people learn |
| **Invrisil** | **DEAL** | measured — everything is negotiable, including you |
| **Pallass** | **QUALIFY** | tested — the city is a machine with entry requirements |

## LISCOR — the hometown (built; keep it this)

- **Art:** warm human-scale stone, amber lights, mud-and-market
  streets; buildings you can see over. Rain-washed (canon; a rain
  weather card is Liscor's one missing signature — future juice).
- **Population:** Drakes + Gnolls + a few Humans; individuals, not
  crowds — EVERY named NPC is somebody you'll talk to twice.
- **Mechanical dialect:** relationships + repeatable home loops
  (pools, stages, chores, boards, your own bed). Systems reward
  RETURNING.
- **Quest register:** neighbor problems. Stakes are personal
  (a botched order, a cellar dispute). Even the dungeon is
  framed as *under our streets*.
- **Sound:** hearth crackle, market murmur, rain later; the inn's
  music theme is the emotional home key.
- **Never:** crowds of extras, monumental architecture, anonymous
  vendors. If Liscor gets bigger, it gets DEEPER (interiors), not wider.

## INVRISIL — the marketplace of ambitions (spec'd, 8c)

- **Art:** pale marble + coin-gold accents, TALL facades (2-cell
  fronts — the first map where buildings loom), lamp rows, glass.
  Vertical LINES, horizontal sprawl. Light: cool daylight, warm
  expensive interiors — wealth reads as light you can't afford.
- **Population:** Human-majority CROWDS — the extras debut (walk-on
  density is itself the message: nobody knows you here). Fashion
  variance; the Brothers' shabby-gentility reads AGAINST it.
- **Mechanical dialect:** transaction + information + stealth.
  Reputation is purchasable, information has prices (the fixer's
  tea-cup rumors), routes have tolls, and the alleys are the
  stealth playground. Economy and sneak systems carry the city.
- **Quest register:** schemes. Multi-party interests, a true villain
  with a respectable face, morally forked endings. Nothing is a
  neighbor problem; everything is leverage.
- **Sound:** carriage wheels, coin-purse foley, string-forward music,
  crowd wash that never fully quiets.
- **Never:** cozy. Even kindness in Invrisil is an investment (the
  Brothers' honor is a BRAND). No free meals.

## PALLASS — the machine city (NEW seed, 8f)

- **Art:** THE VERTICAL CITY — canon's nine-tiered Walled City of
  Inventions rendered as stacked map BANDS: each Pallass map is a
  TIER (a horizontal slice), connected by the great elevators and
  stair-forges. Palette: forge-bronze, slate, steam-white; hard
  geometric masonry (a new tile family — engineered, not laid);
  light = industrial (furnace glow, crystal lamps in ruler-straight
  rows — nothing flickers unless it's a forge).
- **Population:** Drake-supermajority, uniformed everything (guard
  rotations, guild sashes, engineer aprons); Gnolls present,
  Humans RARE and noticed (a Human PC gets pool-line friction —
  the race-variant dialogue seam's best use; Drake PCs get the
  inverse: assumed-local).
- **Mechanical dialect:** VERTICALITY + CREDENTIALS. Movement between
  tiers is gated (permits, guild sponsorship, elevator fees — the
  door_when machinery as civic bureaucracy); the city is a traversal
  PUZZLE (which tier, which lift, whose stamp). Inventions as
  interactables (the observe/skill pillar feasting: devices to
  study, a public forge to use, the game's best [Tactician]/[Engineer]
  content). Skills-wave movement verbs get their cathedral.
- **Quest register:** bureaucratic + industrial — a permit that won't
  issue, a device that shouldn't exist, a guild demarcation war.
  Stakes are SYSTEMIC; the comedy is paperwork; the awe is scale.
- **Sound:** hammer rhythm layers (distance-mixed), steam vents,
  elevator bells; music: percussion-forward, almost martial.
- **Dialogue register:** titles and precision ("Fourth-Tier
  Journeyman Emess, Brasswork. State your business and your
  sponsor."). Warmth exists but must be EARNED past the uniform.
- **Never:** haggling (prices are POSTED — the anti-Invrisil),
  anonymous architecture (every tier has a function), casual entry
  (arriving in Pallass should itself be a quest).

## The distinctness disciplines (bind all city work)

1. **Verb-first:** every new city surface names which verb it serves
   before it's built. A Pallass market stall isn't Invrisil's —
   posted prices, a queue, a permit check.
2. **Palette quarantine:** the three palettes never share accent hues
   (Liscor amber / Invrisil coin-gold-on-marble / Pallass
   forge-bronze-on-slate). Direction cards enforce.
3. **Population texture is mechanics:** extras density (none/crowds/
   uniformed rotations) is a designed value per city, not dressing.
4. **One traversal signature each:** Liscor = doors into depth;
   Invrisil = alleys around fronts; Pallass = LIFTS between tiers.
5. **The PC's race reads differently in each** (the char-creation
   investment pays here): Liscor = whoever you are, you can belong;
   Invrisil = Human-default, others exotic; Pallass = Drake-default,
   Humans watched. Pool-variant lines, never mechanics.
6. **Music keys:** home / strings / percussion. An eyes-closed test
   should place you in the right city in four bars.

CELEBRATED canon artery — our 8a door attunes to it late); v1 scope
