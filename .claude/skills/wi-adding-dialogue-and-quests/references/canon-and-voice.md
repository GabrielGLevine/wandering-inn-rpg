# Canon and voice delivery

New content may use material entering the story no later than the Book 17 slice
defined in `docs/design/spoiler-cutoff.md`; advertised/grandfathered exceptions
remain as documented, but do not create new exceptions by analogy. Check both
the fact and when it appears. Use the in-game sanctioned name when a later canon
name crosses the cutoff.

Create or update a wiki-supported character profile before writing a new
character. Treat its species, palette, silhouette, facts, and voice notes as the
writing contract. Search every existing surface for contradictions before
delivery.

Read lines aloud. Prefer concrete objects, contractions and vocabulary that fit
the speaker, specific humor, and resident knowledge. Remove generic literary
phrases, inflated emotional labels, rhetorical-question openings, repeated
three-part cadence, symmetrical sentence rhythms, “somehow,” unnecessary
intensifiers, and explanatory continuations that restate the preceding clause.
An em dash marks a real interruption or turn and occurs at most once in a line;
most lines need none. Search literal em dashes, escaped Unicode, and ASCII
double-hyphen forms. UI label separators are outside the spoken-line rule.

For Gnoll speech, `, yes?` is available as occasional verbal texture. Keep
`Hrr.` standalone, capitalized, and sparse rather than using it as a prefix on
every line.

Windowed delivery reads the full panel for clipping, option order, occlusion,
speaker identity, and whether the line actually appeared. Compare rendered text
to event payloads; text present only in logs is a defect.
